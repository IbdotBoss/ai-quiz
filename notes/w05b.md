# Genetic Algorithms

## What they are
A genetic algorithm is an optimisation method that imitates biological evolution and survival of the fittest. It works on a population of candidate solutions and improves it over generations. It suits large search spaces such as scheduling, route optimisation and finance.

## Biological terms
- A chromosome is a string of DNA. In the algorithm it is one candidate solution, often a bitstring.
- A gene is one block of the chromosome, such as a single bit of 0 or 1.
- Fitness is how successful an individual is, here how good the solution is.
- Crossover recombines genes from two parents.
- Mutation is a random change, like a copying error.

## The loop
1. Create an initial population of random solutions.
2. Test each solution for fitness and rank them.
3. Remove some bad solutions and duplicate some good ones.
4. Make small changes using crossover and mutation.
5. Repeat until the best solution is good enough.

Problems are usually set up as finding a minimum, so other problems are reformulated as minimisation.

## Why not other methods
The analytical method differentiates an equation and finds where the gradient is zero. It fails when the relationship cannot be written as an equation, when there are several minima, or when the problem is complex with many variables.

Blind generate and test guesses solutions and checks them. It may take too long, can be misled by several minima, and does not build on earlier guesses.

A genetic algorithm gives a guided search. It keeps good solutions and varies them.

## Strengths and limits
| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Advantage | Approximate solutions can usually be found |
| Disadvantage | It can be hard to represent a problem as a genetic problem |
| Best use | Large search spaces |
