# What AI is and the main types of AI

## Defining artificial intelligence
**What it is.** John McCarthy coined the term in 1956. He described AI as the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs. The aim is to use computers to do things we associate with human intelligence, but the machine does not have to work the way a human brain works.

**Why it is hard to define.** The two words are not equally difficult. Artificial means produced by human effort rather than arising naturally, and people broadly agree on that. Intelligence means intellect, wisdom and understanding, and it is hard to define because it is hard to measure. There is no single test and no single score.

**Easily confused.** Many people think AI means copying the human brain. It does not. A program may reach intelligent results by methods that look nothing like human thought, and that still counts.

## The Turing Test
**What it is.** Alan Turing asked whether machines can think. Thinking is too vague to test, so he replaced the question with one that can be tested, which is whether a machine can pass a behaviour test for intelligence.

**How it works.** A human judge communicates with a hidden entity and has to decide whether it is a computer or a person. If the judge cannot tell, the computer has passed and is said to be intelligent.

**Example.** A judge types questions to a hidden party and cannot reliably tell whether a machine or a person is answering. The machine has passed.

**Easily confused.** Passing does not prove consciousness or self-awareness, so it does not make a machine strong AI. The test measures behaviour equivalent to intelligent behaviour, and nothing more.

## Multiple intelligences
**What it is.** The theory of multiple intelligences says human intelligence splits into separate kinds. It does not treat intelligence as one general ability. So whether a machine is intelligent depends on which kind is meant.

**The kinds, with an example for each.**
- Visual-spatial is spatial judgement and picturing things in the mind, such as navigating or seeing an object from another angle.
- Emotional or interpersonal is perceiving and handling emotions, and being sensitive to other people's moods and motives so that you can work in a group.
- Intrapersonal is introspection and a deep understanding of oneself.
- Linguistic-verbal is skill with words, such as reading, writing and telling stories.
- Logical-mathematical is logic, reasoning, numbers and understanding how a causal system works.
- Bodily-kinesthetic is control of the body and skilful handling of objects.
- Naturalistic is identifying, classifying and manipulating elements of the environment such as animals and plants. A Mars rover that classifies rocks resembles it.
- Existential is asking about the meaning of life and our place in the world.

**Easily confused.** Interpersonal looks outward at other people. Intrapersonal looks inward at the self.

## Weak AI and strong AI
**What they are.** Weak AI has limited functionality. It is an algorithm designed to solve a specific problem, using predefined instructions and parameters to produce output, and it does not cover the full range of human cognitive abilities. Strong AI would have intelligence equal to humans, with self-awareness, consciousness, creativity, learning and planning for the future. It aims to be indistinguishable from the human mind.

**Where each stands.** Almost all of today's AI is weak AI. Siri, Alexa, chatbots and self driving cars are examples. Strong AI exists only as a theoretical concept with current technology. Textbooks usually call weak AI narrow AI and mean the same thing.

**Easily confused.** Weak does not mean simple. A self driving car is very capable, but it is built for one job. Strong refers to breadth and human-level ability.

## Four types of AI
This list orders AI by memory and understanding, from simple to advanced.

**Reactive machines.** They have no memory and each does one task, so an input always gives the same output. They rely on statistics, static rules and pattern recognition. Deep Blue, the chess program, and recommendation systems are examples.

**Limited memory machines.** These can look into the past and track objects or situations over time. They imitate the way neurons in the brain work together, so they get better as they receive more data to train on. Deep learning systems such as ChatGPT are examples.

**Theory of mind.** This type would understand human motives and reasoning, grasp intentions and predict behaviour, as if it had human relationships. It can place information in context and works across a broad range of tasks, where classic machine learning works only in one domain. Here it is labelled AGI, although textbooks usually define AGI as general ability and not as this specific type.

**Self-awareness.** A self-aware system would have a sense of self and a conscious understanding of its own existence. It would understand the world and also itself. It does not exist, partly because we do not know enough about the human brain and emotion to build it.

**Easily confused.** Reactive machines and limited memory machines both match patterns. The difference is memory. Theory of mind goes a step further because it tries to understand what the patterns mean, not only to match them.

## How the two splits fit together
Weak and strong AI divide AI by how broad its ability is. The four types divide it by memory and understanding. They are separate lists. Reactive machines are not a third option beside weak and strong. Today's systems are weak AI and fall in the first two types.

## The computing definition of AI
In a computing context, AI is the ability of a machine to mimic the behaviour of living things, mainly humans. How it does this does not matter. Only the outcome matters, which is the behaviour. This follows from the Turing Test, which avoids the unanswerable question of whether a machine really thinks.
