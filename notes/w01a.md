# What AI is and the main types of AI

## Defining AI
The term was coined by John McCarthy in 1956. AI is the science and engineering of making intelligent machines. Artificial means produced by human effort and most people agree on that. Intelligence is very hard to define because it is hard to measure. AI does not have to copy biologically observable methods.

## The Turing Test
Turing replaced the question of whether machines can think with a testable one. Can a machine pass a behaviour test for intelligence? A human judge talks to a hidden entity. If the judge cannot tell whether it is a computer, the computer passes and is said to be intelligent. The test cares about behaviour and not about how the machine works.

## Multiple intelligences
The theory says intelligence is split into modalities and is not one general ability. The types are visual-spatial, emotional or interpersonal, intrapersonal, linguistic-verbal, logical-mathematical, bodily-kinesthetic, naturalistic and existential. Each raises the question of whether a machine could have it.

## Weak AI and strong AI
Weak AI solves a specific problem with predefined instructions and parameters. Almost all of today's AI is weak, such as Siri, Alexa, chatbots and self driving cars. Strong AI would equal human intelligence, with self awareness, consciousness, creativity and planning. It only exists as a theoretical idea.

## Four types of AI
| Type | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Reactive machines | No memory, so the same input gives the same output, as in Deep Blue |
| Limited memory | Looks at the past and improves with more training data, as in deep learning |
| Theory of mind | Understands human motives and intentions and predicts behaviour |
| Self-awareness | Has a sense of self and does not exist yet |

## How the splits differ
Weak and strong AI split AI by how broad its ability is. The four types split it by memory and understanding. Do not mix the two lists.

## Conclusion
In a computing context AI is the ability of a machine to mimic the behaviour of living things, mainly humans. How it does this is not important and the outcome is what counts.
