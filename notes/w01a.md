# What AI is and the types of AI

## Defining artificial intelligence
John McCarthy coined the term in 1956 and described **artificial intelligence** as the science and engineering of making intelligent machines, especially intelligent computer programs. The aim is for computers to do things we link with human intelligence. The machine does not have to work the way a brain works. In computing, AI is the ability of a machine to mimic the behaviour of living things, mainly humans, and only the outcome counts.

The term is hard to define because its two words are not equally clear. **Artificial** means made by human effort, and people agree on that. **Intelligence** means intellect, wisdom and understanding, and it is hard to define because it is hard to measure. There is no single test and no single score.

> A program can reach intelligent results by methods that look nothing like human thought, and that still counts as AI.

## The Turing Test
Alan Turing asked whether machines can think. Thinking is too vague to test, so he replaced the question with one that can be tested, whether a machine can pass a **behaviour test** for intelligence. A human **judge** communicates with a hidden entity and must decide whether it is a computer or a person. If the judge cannot tell, the computer has passed and is said to be intelligent.

> Passing shows behaviour that looks intelligent. It does not prove consciousness, so it does not make a machine strong AI.

## Weak AI and strong AI
**Weak AI**, also called narrow AI, is built to solve one specific problem. It follows predefined instructions and parameters and does not cover the full range of human thinking. Siri, Alexa, chatbots and self-driving cars are weak AI, and almost all AI today is weak. **Strong AI** would match human intelligence, with self-awareness, consciousness, creativity, learning and planning, so it would be indistinguishable from a human mind. It exists only as a theory.

> Weak does not mean simple. A self-driving car is very capable, but it is built for one job. Strong is about breadth.

## The four types of AI
The four types order AI by memory and understanding, from simple to advanced.

1. **Reactive machines** have no memory and do one task, so the same input always gives the same output. They use statistics, fixed rules and pattern matching. Deep Blue, the chess program, and recommendation systems are examples.
2. **Limited memory machines** can look into the past and track things over time. They learn like connected neurons, so they improve with more training data. Deep learning systems such as ChatGPT are examples.
3. **Theory of mind** AI would understand human motives and reasoning, grasp intentions and predict behaviour. It would place information in context and work across many tasks, where classic machine learning works in one domain.
4. **Self-aware** AI would have a sense of self and understand its own existence. It does not exist, partly because we do not know enough about the brain and emotion to build it.

> Weak and strong is a split by breadth of ability. The four types are a split by memory and understanding. Today's systems are weak AI and sit in the first two types.
