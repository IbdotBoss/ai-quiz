# Exploratory data analysis

## What EDA is and why it comes first
**Exploratory data analysis**, or EDA, is the step where you profile, summarise and visualise a dataset before building a model. It shows the structure and quality of the data and the patterns and problems in it. EDA finds **missing values**, duplicates, **outliers**, wrong data types and entry errors. It shows distributions, skew and class imbalance, which decide the transforms and resampling you need. It also guides the choice of model, sets a realistic baseline and flags leakage.

Three techniques come up most.

- **Descriptive statistics** such as mean, median, standard deviation, minimum and maximum show the distribution and spread of each numerical feature.
- **Visualisation** with histograms and box plots shows the shape of a distribution, its skew and its outliers. A **box plot** marks points beyond the whiskers as possible outliers.
- **Correlation analysis** shows how strongly numerical variables move together. A coefficient near +1 or -1 is a strong linear relationship, and near 0 is weak. A value of -0.85 is a strong negative linear relationship.

Too little EDA leaves these problems hidden. The model then trains on bad data, gives misleading results and predicts poorly.

> EDA cannot tell you how well a model will perform. It prepares the data, and testing comes later.

## Missing values and outliers
For **missing values**, find which columns have gaps, how many, and whether the gaps are linked to the outcome. That decides whether to fill them, called imputation, or drop them. For **outliers**, a rule such as the interquartile range flags values far from the rest, and then each one is inspected. An age of 250 is an entry error and should be fixed or removed. A very high income may be real, so it needs a modelling choice such as a log transform.

> Never delete outliers automatically. Some are errors and some are the most important cases.

## Correlation, leakage and multicollinearity
Two features that are strongly correlated carry the same information. A feature that correlates almost perfectly with the target points to **leakage**, where information from the answer has crept into the inputs. The model looks excellent in testing and fails on new data. **Multicollinearity** means one feature can be predicted from the others. It is measured with the variance inflation factor and fixed by dropping, combining or regularising features.

> Correlation does not show cause. A strong correlation only says two variables move together.

## Class imbalance
**Class imbalance** means one class is much rarer than the other, such as fraud among normal payments. Accuracy then misleads, because always guessing the common class scores high while catching nothing. EDA finds the imbalance through class counts. The responses are **stratified splits**, the **F1 score** or PR-AUC instead of accuracy, class weights, or resampling.

> Never delete the rare class to balance the data. It is usually the class that matters.
