# Data modalities and exploratory data analysis

## AI as research
**Why AI helps research.** It finds hidden patterns in large datasets, improves forecasting, automates routine work such as cleaning and labelling data, links fields such as biology and transport, and supports open science through shared models and data.

**The scientific method.** The steps run in a fixed order. Observation identifies a real problem, such as poor air quality. Hypothesis proposes an algorithm or model to tackle it. Experimentation tests it under controlled conditions on datasets. Analysis judges the results with numbers. Conclusion refines the model, generalises the findings and publishes. Reproducibility and peer review matter.

**The research pipeline.** Problem formulation sets the objectives and research questions. Data collection and preprocessing make the data reliable and ethical. Model development chooses and justifies an algorithm. Evaluation and validation use cross-validation, statistical tests and benchmark comparison to check that the model really works. Knowledge contribution adds a new method, insight or application.

**Easily confused.** Model development picks the algorithm. Evaluation checks it. A question describing cross-validation or benchmarking is about evaluation.

## Data modalities
A modality is the kind of data, and each kind is profiled differently. Tabular data has rows as entities and columns as features, often of mixed types. Time series data is ordered, so autocorrelation matters. Text is profiled by tokens, vocabulary and length. Images are profiled by shape, aspect ratio and channels. Audio is profiled by sampling rate, duration and spectrograms. Video adds frame rate, resolution and colour depth.

## What exploratory data analysis is
**What it is.** Exploratory data analysis, or EDA, is the structured process of profiling, summarising and visualising data before formal modelling. It combines simple statistics such as counts, means and correlations with plots such as histograms, box plots and scatter plots.

**Why it comes first.** EDA reveals missing values, duplicates, outliers, wrong types and entry errors. It shows distributions, skew and class imbalance, which guide transforms and resampling. It guides model choice, sets a realistic baseline and flags leakage and bias.

**Easily confused.** EDA is not model testing, so it cannot tell you final performance. Tuning and validation come after it.

## Tabular data checks
- Schema and integrity. Check structure, types and uniqueness. Duplicated rows after a join suggest the join went wrong, and a constant column carries no signal.
- Missing values. Find which columns have gaps and how many, to plan imputation or dropping and to see whether missingness is linked to the outcome.
- Distributions. Summaries and plots show skew, heavy tails and unrealistic values.
- Outliers. A rule such as the interquartile range flags values far from the rest. Then inspect them. An age of 250 is an entry error. A very high income may be real, so it needs a modelling choice such as a transform, not automatic removal.
- Scaling. Features with very different ranges distort distance-based methods and make optimisation unstable, so they may need standard, robust or power scaling.
- Correlation. Strongly correlated features are redundant. A feature that correlates almost perfectly with the target suggests leakage, meaning information from the answer has crept in. Such a model looks excellent in testing and fails on new data.
- Multicollinearity. It measures how well each feature is explained by the other features, using the variance inflation factor, and shows which variables to drop, combine or regularise. Target relationships differ, since they ask how strongly each feature relates to the outcome.
- Class imbalance. When one class is rare, accuracy misleads because always guessing the common class scores well. Respond with stratified splits, F1, class weights or resampling. Never delete the rare class, since it is usually the one that matters.
- Dimensionality. PCA on standardised data shows how variance concentrates, how far the data can be compressed and which axes are mostly noise. It does not prove causes.

## Choosing train, validation and test splits
A split should match how the data were generated, so that the test honestly imitates real use. Independent rows suit random or stratified splits. Data over time suits time-based splits. Records of the same entity suit grouped splits.

**Example.** Three years of daily sales for forecasting. Random shuffling lets the model train on days after the ones it is tested on, which is unrealistic. With many records per patient, a random split puts the same patient in both sets, so the model can recognise the person. A wrong split gives results that look better than reality.

## Time series checks
**What it is.** Observations indexed by time, in order, regular or irregular, with one variable or several. The main tasks are forecasting and anomaly detection.

**Checks.** Continuity and gaps confirm the timeline is sound before resampling or filling. Seasonality finds recurring calendar patterns, such as more airline passengers in the summer holidays. Stationarity asks whether the mean and variance stay stable over time. A series with a rising average is not stationary, and differencing, detrending or a log transform can fix that. Autocorrelation shows how far current values depend on past ones. Change points separate a short spike, which may be noise, from a lasting level change, which means the regime shifted.

## Text checks
Text is sequences of characters or tokens carrying meaning. Stop-word removal drops very frequent words such as the and and, which add noise in bag-of-words models. Case normalisation, usually to lowercase, merges AI and ai and reduces sparsity, but capitalisation can carry meaning. Cleansing standardises text, for example by unescaping HTML and masking URLs, and must not change what the text says. Also check corpus size and empty texts, length distributions, vocabulary, and bigrams and trigrams, because phrases carry meaning that single-word counts lose. Embeddings projected to fewer dimensions reveal clusters and outliers, and give early evidence of how separable the labels are.

## Image checks
An image is an array of pixel values with one or more channels. Profile resolution and aspect ratio to decide on resizing and padding. Check channel statistics, blur, exposure and corrupt files. Look for exact and near-duplicate images across splits using hashes or embeddings. A duplicate in both training and test sets means the model has already seen the test picture, so the results are inflated.
